Before looking at my assessments, I wanted to understand where I started, what changed, and where I am now.

The truth isn't just what makes the headline, it's what never does.

Before

What I thought

At the beginning, I thought that media bias meant that a news source gave false information. When seeing news, I only thought about whether the information was true or not rather than how the story was presented.

Task 1

Circle of Viewpoints

How can media coverage shape different perspectives about the same event?

Point The BBC's coverage on Somalia can be viewed differently depending on which perspective sees it.
Evidence From the parent's perspective, the coverage focuses too much on catastrophe and overlooks the resilience of Somali people. From the reporter's perspective, dramatic and tense details such as “eight gunmen” are needed to show the danger and capture the audience’s attention. From the audience’s perspective, images of armed convoys and destroyed buildings create fear and sympathy, but may make Somalia seem like nothing but an endless warzone.
Explanation These three different viewpoints show that media coverage doesn't affect everyone the same way. The parent feels misrepresented, the reporter feels the responsibility to focus on danger to create dramatic news, and the audience can form a limited perception of Somalia. Therefore, different people and different perspectives can percieve the same news in different ways.

My Reflection

Doing this activity taught me how challenging it would be to understand an issue from other perspectives that are different from my own. When me and my team took on the role of parent, reporter and audience, we had to think about what each person would feel about BBC's coverage.

02 / ASSESSMENT

Behind the Scenes

This is the thinking process behind my answers, as I analyzed different aspects and layers of the single clip.

Language Use I noticed that the language used in the scene I chose highlighted the violence and conflict, and I realized that it may lead the audience to form stereotypes about Somalia.
↓
Emotional Appeal When I saw the scene, the stressed locals holding their children made me feel empathy and sadness. This feeling is what is intended by the news reporters.
↓
Visual Footage I looked at the scene and saw the women and children, along with the ruins. I think that this is an intentional choice by the news reporters to create a feeling of fear and sadness.
↓
Credibility of Sources As the news was published by BBC and had local citizens involved, I thought that the news was trustworthy. Despite that, the lack of other perspectives shown made me feel like the audience will only hear one side of the story.
↓
The Overall Media Bias After analyzing all of the different media framing elements, I came to the conclusion that negative media framing is indeed present in this clip, affecting the audience's perception.

Formative Assessment 03

The Final Test

For this assignment, I had to create a Ted-style talk that identified media bias in a news story.

Point The Guardian uses critical language and selection of information to portray Charlie Kirk in a negative light, making the article biased against him.
Evidence The article describes Kirk using words such as "polarizing", "misinformation", "racist", "silence others", and "hate-inflicted speech". Besides that, the article focuses heavily on the contreversies surrounding Kirk, paying little attention to his career achievements and positive contributions.
Explanation These words have negative connotations and can influence readers to associate Kirk with negative and harmful behaviour. At the same time, giving less attention to other parts of his career means readers have an imbalanced picture of his life.
